# Audience

Who we are for. Who we are not for. What they look like, what they spend their day doing, and what to say to them.

---

## Primary persona — the owner-operator seller

**Who they are:**
- Owns a small business in Israel.
- Sells to consumers or other small businesses.
- Runs their own sales — they are not "the founder" with a sales team; they *are* the sales team.
- Often the only person between a new lead and a closed deal.

**Their day:**
- Wakes up to 5–20 messages across WhatsApp, Instagram, and Facebook.
- Drives, meets clients, takes calls, sends quotes, runs errands.
- Tries to track everything in their head and on their phone.
- Loses leads not because they don't care, but because there is no system.

**What they already use:**
- WhatsApp Business (or regular WhatsApp).
- Google Sheets or Excel.
- Notes app on iPhone or Android.
- A notebook on the desk.
- Maybe a Google Calendar reminder.

**What they tried that failed:**
- Monday CRM — too complex, abandoned in week two.
- PowerLink / Salesforce — quoted ₪2,000+/month, walked away.
- A spreadsheet system a friend built for them — broke when the friend stopped supporting it.

**What they say:**
- "אני יודע שאני מפספס לידים. אני לא יודע כמה."
- "כל פעם שאני פותח את הוואטסאפ אני מתחיל מהתחלה."
- "ניסיתי מאנדיי. ויתרתי אחרי שבוע."
- "אני צריך משהו פשוט."

---

## Secondary persona — the small sales team (2–8 reps)

**Who they are:**
- A small agency or business with a manager plus 2–8 sales reps.
- Reps work in parallel, sometimes splitting the same deal.
- The manager wants visibility without micromanaging.

**Their pain on top of the primary pain:**
- "Who is working this lead?" → no answer.
- "Whose commission is this?" → argued out at end of month.
- Onboarding a new rep → six weeks of "ask in the group chat."
- Losing a rep → their leads disappear with them.

**What they need that the primary persona does not:**
- Lead assignment and reassignment.
- Multi-rep deals with percentage splits.
- A way to transfer all of someone's work when they leave.
- Manager-level visibility across the team.
- Internal competitions to keep reps motivated.

All of which already exist in the product.

## Industries we naturally fit

Any business that receives inbound leads on social channels and closes them by phone, message, or in-person:

- Real estate brokers and small agencies
- Mortgage and finance consultants
- Cosmetic and aesthetic clinics
- Personal trainers and small studios
- Photographers, videographers, event vendors
- Small construction, renovation, and contractor businesses
- Driving schools and private course providers
- Coaches and consultants
- Small marketing agencies

We are not industry-locked. The schema deliberately uses generic branches (Services, Products, Courses, Subscriptions, Packages, Consulting, Other) so any of the above fits.

---

## Industries we are *not* for

- Insurance brokers needing policy lifecycle management (the previous schema; we left it behind).
- Enterprise B2B with 6+ month sales cycles and procurement processes.
- E-commerce with thousands of orders/day (we are not a Shopify CRM).
- Field service / dispatch (we don't route technicians).
- Customer support teams (no ticketing).

If they ask, send them elsewhere. Wrong-fit users churn fast and hurt the brand.

---

## Geography & language

- **Primary market:** Israel.
- **Language:** Hebrew, RTL.
- **Hours of use:** Morning (9–11), late afternoon (16–18). Plan emails and notifications accordingly.

We are not localizing to English first. The advantage is being the *best* Hebrew product, not the *most international* one.

---

## Buyer vs. user

For the primary persona, buyer = user. They sign up, they pay, they use it.

For the secondary persona:
- **Buyer:** the manager or owner who feels the pain of "I can't see what my team is doing."
- **User:** the reps, who feel "this is finally not Excel."
- **Champion:** usually the manager. Sell the manager. Make sure the reps don't hate it.

---

## What they read, watch, and listen to

- Local business Facebook groups.
- WhatsApp broadcast lists from business coaches.
- Reels and short videos from Israeli sales/marketing influencers.
- Podcasts on small business growth (in Hebrew).
- LinkedIn — but rarely, and skeptically.

Plan content distribution accordingly. Long-form English LinkedIn posts are the wrong medium. Short Hebrew Reels with a real screen recording are the right medium.

---

## The wrong audience signals (refuse to sell)

If a prospect asks any of these, they are not for us:

- "Do you integrate with SAP / Oracle / Salesforce?"
- "Can we customize the data model?"
- "Do you have a procurement and security review process?"
- "Can we run this on-premise?"
- "How do you handle multi-region compliance?"

Polite no. Suggest they look at PowerLink or Monday. They will churn from us anyway and burn support cycles on the way out.
